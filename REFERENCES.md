Every study, review, and guideline behind Myaku, grouped by the part of the app it shapes. The same list is in the app at `references.html`.

## Body

Sleep, heart rate, and what a wearable can and cannot tell you.

- Milewski, Skaggs, Bishop, and colleagues (2014). [Chronic lack of sleep is associated with increased sports injuries in adolescent athletes.](https://journals.lww.com/pedorthopaedics/fulltext/2014/03000/chronic_lack_of_sleep_is_associated_with_increased.1.aspx) *Journal of Pediatric Orthopaedics, 34(2), 129–133*.  
  Athletes sleeping under eight hours were 1.7 times as likely to be injured, which is behind the sleep line on Body.

- American Academy of Sleep Medicine (2016). [Teen sleep duration health advisory.](https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/teen-sleep-duration-health-advisory/) *Position statement, updated April 2016*.  
  Eight to ten hours for ages 13 to 18, the sleep goal offered to high school athletes.

- Mah, Mah, Kezirian, and colleagues (2011). [The effects of sleep extension on the athletic performance of collegiate basketball players.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21731144/) *Sleep, 34(7), 943–950*.  
  More sleep came with faster sprints and better shooting, which is why Body treats sleep as part of training.

- Phillips, Clerx, O'Brien, and colleagues (2017). [Irregular sleep/wake patterns are associated with poorer academic performance and delayed circadian and sleep/wake timing.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28607474/) *Scientific Reports, 7, 3216*.  
  Irregular sleep went with lower grades even at the same total, which is behind the regularity reading on Body.

- Buchheit (2014). [Monitoring training status with HR measures: do all roads lead to Rome?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3936188/) *Frontiers in Physiology, 5, 73*.  
  What heart rate variability and resting heart rate can and cannot say about training, behind both readings on Body.

## Brain

The three-minute reaction test and what can throw it off.

- Basner, Mollicone, and Dinges (2011). [Validity and sensitivity of a brief psychomotor vigilance test (PVT-B) to total and partial sleep deprivation.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22025811/) *Acta Astronautica, 69(11–12), 949–959*.  
  The three-minute reaction test Brain runs, including the 355 millisecond line for a lapse.

- Antler, Yamazaki, Casale, and colleagues (2022). [The 3-minute psychomotor vigilance test demonstrates inadequate convergent validity relative to the 10-minute psychomotor vigilance test across sleep loss and recovery.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.815697/full) *Frontiers in Neuroscience, 16, 815697*.  
  The short test tracks the long one only loosely, which is why Brain compares weeks and gives speed the larger say.

- Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington, and colleagues (2003). [The cumulative cost of additional wakefulness: dose-response effects on neurobehavioral functions and sleep physiology from chronic sleep restriction and total sleep deprivation.](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article-abstract/26/2/117/2709164) *Sleep, 26(2), 117–126*.  
  Reaction time kept getting worse under short sleep while people stopped feeling it, which is why Brain measures rather than asks.

- Kamimori, McLellan, Tate, and colleagues (2015). [Caffeine improves reaction time, vigilance and logical reasoning during extended periods with restricted opportunities for sleep.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4432086/) *Psychopharmacology, 232(12), 2031–2042*.  
  Caffeine can hide fatigue on the test, which is why a dose before a session is noted.

- Basner, Hermosillo, Nasrini, and colleagues (2018). [Repeated administration effects on psychomotor vigilance test performance.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29126328/) *Sleep, 41(1)*.  
  Scores improve with practice, so a change across weeks is not mistaken for getting used to the test.

- Nicosia, Wang, Aschenbrenner, and colleagues (2023). [To BYOD or not: are device latencies important for bring-your-own-device (BYOD) smartphone cognitive testing?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9918597/) *Behavior Research Methods, 55(6), 2800–2812*.  
  Phones differ by up to about 100 milliseconds in touch and display delay, which is why Brain records the device.

## Life

Check-ins, the weekly reflection, and what the Life channel reads.

- Saw, Main, and Gastin (2016). [Monitoring the athlete training response: subjective self-reported measures trump commonly used objective measures: a systematic review.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26423706/) *British Journal of Sports Medicine, 50(5), 281–291*.  
  Self-report responded to training more sensitively than objective markers, which is why Life counts as much as Body and Brain.

- Karasek (1979). [Job demands, job decision latitude, and mental strain: implications for job redesign.](https://doi.org/10.2307/2392498) *Administrative Science Quarterly, 24(2), 285–308*.  
  The original demand and control model behind the strain corner.

- Kain and Jex (2010). [Karasek's (1979) job demands-control model: a summary of current issues and recommendations for future research.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242179592_Karasek's_1979_job_demands-control_model_A_summary_of_current_issues_and_recommendations_for_future_research) *Research in Occupational Stress and Well-being, 8, 237–268*.  
  What has held up about that model since, cited on Life.

- Raedeke and Smith (2001). [Development and preliminary validation of an athlete burnout measure.](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/jsep/23/4/article-p281.xml) *Journal of Sport and Exercise Psychology, 23(4), 281–306*.  
  The three burnout signs asked in the weekly reflection.

- Dišlere, Mārtinsone, Koļesņikova, and colleagues (2025). [A scoping review of longitudinal studies of athlete burnout.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1502174/full) *Frontiers in Psychology, 16, 1502174*.  
  How burnout develops over a season, cited on Life.

- Li, Wang, Pyun, and Kee (2013). [Burnout and its relations with basic psychological needs and motivation among athletes: a systematic review and meta-analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychsport.2013.04.009) *Psychology of Sport and Exercise, 14(5), 692–700*.  
  Unmet needs for control, competence, and connection go with burnout, which is why feeling connected now counts.

- Monteiro, Cid, Teixeira, and colleagues (2020). [Understanding needs satisfaction and frustration in young athletes: factor structure and invariance analysis.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7312040/) *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(11), 4046*.  
  Needs being met and needs being frustrated behave as separate things in athletes, not two ends of one scale.

- Russell (1980). [A circumplex model of affect.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077714) *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 39(6), 1161–1178*.  
  The two directions of the mood square, unpleasant to pleasant and calm to wired.

- Russell, Weiss, and Mendelsohn (1989). [Affect grid: a single-item scale of pleasure and arousal.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.57.3.493) *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 57(3), 493–502*.  
  Mood answered as one point on a grid, as the check-in and journal ask it.

- Saris, Revilla, Krosnick, and Shaeffer (2010). [Comparing questions with agree/disagree response options to questions with item-specific response options.](https://doi.org/10.18148/srm/2010.v4i1.2682) *Survey Research Methods, 4(1), 61–79*.  
  Answers written for the specific question gave better data, which is why every reflection square has its own words.

- Mao (2025). [Advancements in research on psychological and emotional aspects of student-athletes.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12540375/) *Frontiers in Psychology, 16, 1645177*.  
  Training load, academics, and coach or team relations were the stressors studies reported most, which is why the reader tags coach and team apart from training.

## Journal Prompts

Where each kind of entry comes from.

- Frattaroli (2006). [Experimental disclosure and its moderators: a meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073523/) *Psychological Bulletin, 132(6), 823–865*.  
  Get It Out. Expressive writing helps on average across 146 trials, but the effect is small.

- Kross and Ayduk (2008). [Facilitating adaptive emotional analysis: distinguishing distanced-analysis of depressive experiences from immersed-analysis and distraction.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167208315938) *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 34(7), 924–938*.  
  Step Outside It. Reflecting from a distance lowered distress compared with reliving it from inside.

- Park, Ayduk, and Kross (2016). [Stepping back to move forward: expressive writing promotes self-distancing.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26461252/) *Emotion, 16(3), 349–364*.  
  Step Outside It. Writing itself can create that distance.

- Seligman, Steen, Park, and Peterson (2005). [Positive psychology progress: empirical validation of interventions.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045394/) *American Psychologist, 60(5), 410–421*.  
  Three Good Things. A week of it raised wellbeing six months later.

- Emmons and McCullough (2003). [Counting blessings versus burdens: an experimental investigation of gratitude and subjective well-being in daily life.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12585811/) *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 84(2), 377–389*.  
  Three Good Things. The weekly gratitude version that improved wellbeing.

- Lyubomirsky, Sheldon, and Schkade (2005). [Pursuing happiness: the architecture of sustainable change.](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.9.2.111) *Review of General Psychology, 9(2), 111–131*.  
  Three Good Things. Once a week worked better than three times a week, which is why it is suggested weekly.

- Scullin, Krueger, Ballard, and colleagues (2018). [The effects of bedtime writing on difficulty falling asleep: a polysomnographic study comparing to-do lists and completed activity lists.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/) *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 147(1), 139–146*.  
  Tomorrow's List. A specific to-do list before bed came with falling asleep about nine minutes faster.

- Treynor, Gonzalez, and Nolen-Hoeksema (2003). [Rumination reconsidered: a psychometric analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023910315561) *Cognitive Therapy and Research, 27(3), 247–259*.  
  Brooding and reflecting are different, which is why no prompt asks why you feel this way and the journal watches for hard entries that circle.

## Reading Entries

Reading journal entries on your own computer, and how much to trust it.

- Ringwald, Taxali, Angstadt, and colleagues (2026). [Scalable, context-sensitive psychiatric assessment with large language models and brief diaries.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13439252/) *Psychological Medicine, 56, e231*.  
  Model readings of daily diaries agreed with self-reports at about .42 across people but .28 day to day, which is why your writing counts for half.

- Soyebo, Donawa, Aguilar Barrios, and colleagues (2026). [On-device language models for privacy-preserving stress prediction: a multimodal evaluation on mobile health.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11961) *arXiv preprint 2609.11961*.  
  Small models running on the device itself are fast enough to read entries without sending them anywhere.

- Eisele, Vachon, Lafit, and colleagues (2022). [The effects of sampling frequency and questionnaire length on perceived burden, compliance, and careless responding in experience sampling data in a student population.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32909448/) *Assessment, 29(2), 136–151*.  
  Longer questionnaires, not more frequent ones, raised burden, which is why the journal counts a day instead of adding questions to it.

- Wrzus and Neubauer (2023). [Ecological momentary assessment: a meta-analysis on designs, samples, and compliance across research fields.](https://doi.org/10.1177/10731911211067538) *Assessment, 30(3), 825–846*.  
  Across 477 studies, how often people were asked did not predict whether they kept answering.

## Caffeine

Timing, dose, and limits.

- Gardiner, Weakley, Burke, and colleagues (2023). [The effect of caffeine on subsequent sleep: a systematic review and meta-analysis.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36870101/) *Sleep Medicine Reviews, 69, 101764*.  
  How much sleep caffeine costs and how timing changes it, behind the bedtime zones on Caffeine.

- Gardiner, Weakley, Burke, and colleagues (2025). [Dose and timing effects of caffeine on subsequent sleep: a randomized clinical crossover trial.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11985402/) *Sleep, 48(4), zsae230*.  
  400 mg twelve hours before bed still cut deep sleep, while 100 mg four hours before made no measurable difference.

- Drake, Roehrs, Shambroom, and colleagues (2013). [Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24235903/) *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 9(11), 1195–1200*.  
  400 mg six hours before bed cost more than an hour of sleep that people did not notice.

- Institute of Medicine (2001). [Caffeine for the sustainment of mental task performance: formulations for military operations.](https://www.nap.edu/catalog/10219) *National Academies Press*.  
  Its pharmacology chapter puts caffeine's half-life near five hours, varying widely between people.

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2024). [Spilling the beans: how much caffeine is too much?](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) *Consumer update*.  
  400 mg a day as the amount not generally linked with harm in healthy adults.

- American Academy of Pediatrics (2011). [Sports drinks and energy drinks for children and adolescents: are they appropriate?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21624882/) *Pediatrics, 127(6), 1182–1189*.  
  Energy drinks have no place in young people's diets. The 100 mg a day ceiling for ages 12 to 18 is the figure widely attributed to the Academy's guidance.

## Trends And Schedule

Workload ratios and the weeks that collide.

- Gabbett (2016). [The training-injury prevention paradox: should athletes be training smarter and harder?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26758673/) *British Journal of Sports Medicine, 50(5), 273–280*.  
  Where the 0.8 to 1.3 workload range on Trends comes from.

- Impellizzeri, Tenan, Kempton, and colleagues (2020). [Acute:chronic workload ratio: conceptual issues and fundamental pitfalls.](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/ijspp/15/6/article-p907.xml) *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance, 15(6), 907–913*.  
  Why Trends shows that range with a warning rather than as a rule.

- Heller, Herzog, Brager, and colleagues (2024). [The negative effects of travel on student athletes through sleep and circadian disruption.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37978840/) *Journal of Biological Rhythms, 39(1), 5–19*.  
  Why travel weighs heavily when the schedule looks for weeks that collide.

- Mann, Bryant, Johnstone, and colleagues (2016). [Effect of physical and academic stress on illness and injury in Division 1 college football players.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049791/) *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 30(1), 20–25*.  
  Illness and injury peaked in exam weeks, which is why exams weigh heavily in the same check.
