How To Read This

Each faint dot is one morning, and the line is the average of the seven days up to that point. A single morning moves with a late meal, a hard session, or a bad night, so the line is the part to watch.

Your normal range comes from the four weeks before this one, set at half of your own usual day-to-day swing. That is a threshold sports scientists commonly use, not one proven to be exactly right. Higher usually means better recovered, and a drop below the range that lasts is the signal worth noticing.

After Hard Days
What Matters For Recovery
Under Eight Hours In a 2014 study, adolescent athletes who slept under eight hours a night were 1.7 times more likely to be injured.
Sleep Need By Age Sleep medicine guidance is 8 to 10 hours for ages 13 to 18, and at least 7 hours for adults.
More Sleep, Better Play College basketball players who extended their sleep sprinted faster, shot more accurately, and reacted faster on the same kind of test Myaku uses.
Regular Schedules Matter In a 2017 study of college students, irregular sleep went with lower grades and a later body clock, even when total sleep was similar.
Read The Average Single mornings of heart rate data are noisy, so a change only shows reliably across several days of readings.
Same Device Only Apple Watch reports a different variability measure than Whoop and Fitbit, so switching devices can look like a change in you.

Based on a 2014 injury study, sleep medicine guidance, a 2011 basketball study, a 2017 regularity study, and a 2014 review of heart rate monitoring.
