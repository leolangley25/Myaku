How Reading Works

The model runs on this computer through Ollama. Your entry goes from this app to that model and nowhere else, and there is no fallback to an online service, so when no model is running an entry simply waits.

It is asked for the same things your check-in asks: how the day felt, how much was being asked of you, whether it was your call, what it was about, and whether any of the three burnout signs came up. You see exactly what it took and can change any of it, or leave an entry out.

A model reading tone from writing agrees with what people report only loosely from day to day, so this counts for half as much as the answers you give directly. It describes the writing, never the writer.

Why These Prompts

A blank page is the reason most journals stop in February, and unstructured venting is the reason some of them make people feel worse. Each prompt here comes from a study, and each one says what it is for.

Writing about the hardest thing helps on average, but the average is small, and people often feel worse immediately afterwards before improving over the following weeks. It is not a treatment and this app is not a therapist.

The prompts avoid the questions that sit on the rumination scale, like asking why you feel this way. When writing about a bad day keeps circling, writing about yourself in the third person tends to settle it better than writing from inside it.
